Problem Statement

Why Heart Disease?

Heart disease is one of the leading causes of death globally. Early detection can save lives — and machine learning models can help doctors flag high-risk patients faster and more accurately.

🎯

The Goal

Build a binary classification system that predicts whether a patient has heart disease (1) or not (0) based on 13 medical attributes — and determine which ML approach works best.

🏥

Real-World Impact

An accurate, interpretable model could assist cardiologists in prioritizing high-risk patients for further testing, reducing diagnostic delays and improving patient outcomes.

⚖️

The Trade-off

This project explores the core tension in medical AI: accuracy vs. interpretability. A model a doctor can explain to a patient may matter more than one that's 2% more accurate.

📊

The Approach

Three models of increasing complexity were trained and evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, and 5-fold cross-validation — giving a complete picture of performance.

🧠 Complexity ≠ Always Better

The Neural Network only outperformed Logistic Regression by ~3.4%. For smaller structured datasets like this, simple models remain highly competitive.

📉 Neural Network Overfit First

The initial Neural Network scored 100% on training data — a classic overfitting case. Early stopping and L2 regularization brought it back down to a realistic generalized performance.

🌳 Decision Tree: Most Reliable

With the lowest cross-validation standard deviation (±1.90%), the Decision Tree was the most consistent performer across different data splits.

📈 Logistic Regression Wins ROC

Despite lower test accuracy, Logistic Regression achieved the highest ROC-AUC score — meaning it ranks patients most reliably by risk level, which matters most in clinical settings.

Takeaways

What I Learned

This project reinforced a key lesson in applied ML: the best model isn't always the most complex one — especially in high-stakes domains like healthcare.

🏥 Key Findings

  • All three models exceeded 80% accuracy on unseen test data
  • Logistic Regression achieved the highest ROC-AUC despite lower raw accuracy
  • Neural Network overfitting was a real challenge, requiring regularization
  • Chest pain type dominated feature importance at 43%
  • Model complexity gap was only ~3.4% — huge cost for tiny gain

🔧 Technical Skills Demonstrated

  • Scikit-learn pipeline: preprocessing, training, evaluation
  • Cross-validation and overfitting prevention
  • ROC curves, confusion matrices, and F1-score interpretation
  • Feature importance analysis and dimensionality insights
  • Python: pandas, numpy, matplotlib, seaborn

💡 Real-World Recommendation

  • In clinical settings, Logistic Regression may be the most valuable model due to its interpretability — doctors can understand and explain predictions to patients
  • Neural Networks require more data to truly outperform simpler models; with larger datasets (100k+ records), the gap would likely widen significantly
  • Future work: remove low-importance features, try ensemble methods (Random Forest, XGBoost), and validate on a diverse held-out population dataset
